# Build three enterprise AI practices as one industrialized capability

**AI Engineering Transformation  |  AI Trust, Risk & Security  |  Managed AI Operations & Assurance**

> Executive blueprint  •  Mandate, architecture, service lifecycles, controls, scale economics and delivery roadmap

<!-- _header: "EXECUTIVE SYNTHESIS" -->

# Make AI delivery scalable without multiplying unmanaged risk

**The case for one integrated service portfolio**

### Why now

AI-enabled engineering and agentic systems are moving from isolated experiments into core enterprise workflows; assurance must scale with adoption.

### What changes

A shared delivery system replaces ad hoc pilots: productized offers, reusable platforms, risk-tiered gates and named control ownership.

### Where value sits

Engineering throughput and modernization; higher-confidence AI adoption; recurring monitoring, assurance and incident response.

> **Decision / principle:** Executive choice: fund a common platform and three accountable practice P&Ls—not three disconnected centers of excellence.

<!-- _header: "EXECUTIVE SYNTHESIS" -->

# Solve the three bottlenecks that block enterprise AI scale

**A deliberately linked architecture turns hard-to-reuse services into a repeatable portfolio**

### AI Engineering Transformation

Solves delivery bottlenecks: unclear requirements, handoffs, technical debt, testing congestion and limited engineering capacity.

### AI Trust, Risk & Security

Solves permission-to-scale bottlenecks: fragmented inventories, unclear accountability, inconsistent evidence and unsafe tool access.

### Managed AI Operations & Assurance

Solves run-state bottlenecks: invisible model behavior, weak incident processes, uncontrolled costs and one-off assessment cycles.

> **Decision / principle:** Scale mechanism: shared control plane + reusable accelerators + certified practitioners + evidence reused across delivery, assurance and operations.

# Separate economic value from vendor productivity claims

**Baseline before benefits; instrument cost, quality and risk together**

### Engineering value

Change lead time; verified throughput; escaped defects; technical-debt retired; avoided rework. Do not use code volume as a proxy.

### Trust value

Faster approval cycles; percent inventory covered; findings remediated; audit time reduced; high-risk exceptions closed.

### Managed value

Lower incident exposure; lower time to detection/recovery; controlled inference costs; fewer surprise failures; higher renewal rates.

> **Decision / principle:** Value = gross improvement – inference / tooling / security / change costs – residual rework. Report confidence intervals and baselines.

# The risk tier determines autonomy, release gates and operations intensity

**Start with three enterprise-defined tiers; map to applicable law and sector rules**

### TIER 1  |  LOW
- Internal drafting, read-only assistance
- Sandboxed tools; routine testing
- Owner sign-off and periodic review

### TIER 2  |  MODERATE
- Customer-facing advisory, sensitive data
- Formal evals; security and privacy review
- Approval for consequential actions; monitoring

### TIER 3  |  HIGH
- Material decisions or autonomous actions
- Independent challenge; strict least privilege
- Human authorization; enhanced logging; stop/rollback

> **Decision / principle:** A risk tier is not the same thing as the EU AI Act’s legal classification. Document the mapping per use case.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 01 / AI ENGINEERING TRANSFORMATION" -->

# Make AI a governed participant across the entire software lifecycle

**Practice executive brief**

### Why it matters

Developers alone are not the constraint: requirements churn, review queues, system knowledge and test reliability dominate delivery economics.

### What changes

Move from isolated code assistants to intent-to-production workflows, verified artifacts, bounded agents and review gates linked to CI/CD.

### Where scale comes from

Reusable specifications, context packs, test harnesses, golden paths, reference agents and shared measurements across application portfolios.

> **Decision / principle:** Offer promise: improve verified delivery outcomes, not lines of AI-generated code. Preserve human ownership of releases.

# AI-DLC integrates Inception, Construction and Operations

**Adapt the AWS three-phase structure without hard-coding a single vendor’s tools**

| Stage | Ownership / activity | Outcome |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1A | Inception | Intent, discovery, reverse engineering |
| 1B | Inception | Requirements + acceptance contracts |
| 2A | Construction | Architecture, NFRs, threat model |

| 2C | Construction | Verification and release gate |
| 3A | Operations | Observe, support and recover |
| 3B | Operations | Feed incidents into context |

> **Decision / principle:** Loops are deliberate: failed evaluations return to requirements/design, not merely another prompt. Operations feedback updates durable assets.

# Treat each engineering stage as an artifact contract

**Gate evidence prevents ambiguous handoffs and agentic drift**

| Gate | Required inputs | Required output | Pass / fail evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| G0: Intake | Value case, repository rights, data policy | Scoped change and risk tier | Owner + data authorization |
| G1: Inception | Domain/context pack, testable intent | Approved spec, ADR, acceptance tests | Traceability + contradiction review |
| G2: Design | NFRs, threat model, dependencies | Architecture, task graph, rollback | Human design review |
| G3: Build | Approved plan + safe tool scope | Small PRs, tests, SBOM / provenance | CI + security gates |
| G4: Release | Verification and risk evidence | Signed release / fallback plan | Quality threshold + approver |
| G5: Operate | Telemetry, SLO and runbook | Incident / change feedback | Blameless RCA + spec updates |

> **Decision / principle:** High-risk changes require independent approvals; acceptance criteria should be testable before code is generated.

# An engineering-agent stack needs bounded context and secure tool access

**Avoid a monolithic coding bot with production credentials**

| Layer | Functions |
|:--|:--|
| Intent & context | Repository index • requirements • ADRs • domain glossary • source provenance • freshness limits |
| Workflow orchestration | Explicit state machine • planner / executor • handoffs • retries • checkpoints • budgets |
| Tool boundary | Scoped read/write actions • sandbox • secret broker • permissioned Git / CI / ticket access |
| Verification plane | Unit / integration / contract / security tests • diff review • reproducible builds • eval suites |
| Delivery & telemetry | PR provenance • CI/CD approvals • change metrics • traces • incident-to-spec feedback |

> **Decision / principle:** Prefer a deterministic workflow for predictable steps; insert agent reasoning only where ambiguity justifies its cost.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 01 / PATTERNS" -->

# Choose the least autonomous agent pattern that solves the task

**A pattern-selection playbook for architecture reviews**

| Pattern | Best-fit use case | Control method | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single agent + tools | Narrow bounded task | Schema, scoped tools, max turns | Tool misuse / hallucinated facts |
| Sequential workflow | Requirements → design → tests | Explicit gates, retries, rollback | Error cascades |
| Planner–executor | Unstructured multi-step change | Approval of plan, task budget | Bad plan amplified |
| Supervisor–workers | Parallel specialized tasks | Delegation bounds, ownership | Coordination and cost explosion |
| Evaluator–optimizer | Iterative quality refinement | Independent eval, stopping rule | Overfitting to evaluator |
| Human-in-the-loop | Irreversible / high-impact actions | Named approval, dual control | Rubber-stamp approvals |

> **Decision / principle:** Distributed multi-agent orchestration is an exception requiring measured advantage over simpler alternatives.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 01 / OPERATIONS" -->

# Staff the AI engineering factory around products—not prompts

**Clear operational responsibilities are necessary for sustained throughput**

### Product + architecture

Product owner owns outcome and acceptance; architect owns design, NFRs, agent boundary and architecture decisions.

### Engineering + platform

Engineers own generated artifacts and integration; platform team owns models, identity, sandbox, observability and policy distribution.

### Quality + security

QE owns test strategy and challenge datasets; AppSec owns threat model and vulnerability response; release owner authorizes deployment.

> **Decision / principle:** Daily control loop: work intake → spec review → agent run → PR verification → release decision → incident learning.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 01 / CONTROLS" -->

# Move from AI-written code to independently verified software

**A complete CI/CD quality stack remains mandatory**

### Pre-merge

Secrets / licence scanning, SAST, dependency checks, provenance, unit/contract tests, agent action logs, architecture checks.

### Release

Integration and performance tests; threat-model deltas; human approval for risk-tier triggers; signed artifacts and rollback plan.

### Post-release

Change-failure monitoring, trace correlation, feedback and bug reports connected to specs; prompt/model changes versioned like code.

> **Decision / principle:** A passing unit test is not proof of correctness; validate authorization, adverse paths, state changes and recovery behavior.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 01 / SCALE" -->

# Industrialize engineering through golden paths and reference kits

**Reusable context and evidence reduce project-by-project reinvention**

### Repeatable patterns

Reference repositories for API, modernization, document workflow and internal tool agents; validated architecture and policy templates.

### Production-grade assets

Golden datasets, acceptance-contract templates, test generators, model routing rules, tool adapters, evaluation dashboards, SLO runbooks.

### Adoption mechanism

Platform product team curates versions; teams contribute improvements; portfolio board retires underperforming patterns and measures reuse.

> **Decision / principle:** Scale arrives when the second team deploys safely faster than the first—not when the first pilot demos impressively.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 01 / GOTCHAS" -->

# Manage the failure modes of AI-native development explicitly

**What delivery leaders should not assume**

### Productivity illusion

Generated code may expand review queues, PR size, defect density or maintenance burden. Measure end-to-end cycle time and escaped defects.

### Context fragility

Outdated repo context, undocumented business rules and misleading retrieval cause consistent but wrong solutions. Version and review sources.

### Security and accountability

Tool access, supply chain, secrets and licence obligations persist. Never allow agents to self-authorize privileged changes.

> **Decision / principle:** Stop conditions: no bounded scope, no test oracle, no rollback path, unsafe data access or no human release owner.

# Turn trusted AI from policy documents into enforceable controls

**Practice executive brief**

### Why it matters

Boards cannot manage models and agents they cannot inventory, classify, evaluate or explain. Risk and regulation differ by context.

### What changes

A system-of-record and lifecycle gates connect design, security, privacy, testing, approval, incidents and third parties.

### Where scale comes from

Reusable control mappings, evidence schemas, automated tests, policy-as-code and managed remediation across AI estates.

> **Decision / principle:** Assurance is a claim supported by scoped evidence—not a claim that an AI system is universally safe or certified.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 02 / RISK" -->

# Approve AI systems based on use, impact and permitted actions

**Illustrative internal tiering—not a substitute for legal risk classification**

### IMPACT / RIGHTS
- Who is affected?
- Are decisions consequential?
- Could errors violate rights or obligations?

### AUTONOMY / ACCESS
- Can the agent take actions?
- Which data and tools are accessible?
- Are effects reversible and bounded?

### CONTROL / ASSURANCE
- What independent evidence exists?
- Who accepts residual risk?
- What alerts, fallback and escalation exist?

> **Decision / principle:** Required result: risk record + authorized scope + control baseline + named owner + expiry / re-review trigger.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 02 / STANDARDS" -->

# Use standards as a control map, not as interchangeable badges

**The applicability of each obligation depends on jurisdiction, role and sector**

| Anchor | What it contributes | Mapped enterprise artifact | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | AI management-system requirements | Policy, governance, management review | Org-level scope; not blanket model safety |
| ISO/IEC 23894:2023 | AI-specific risk guidance | Risk method and mitigation register | Guidance, not standalone certification |
| NIST AI RMF + GenAI Profile | Govern / Map / Measure / Manage | AI assurance and evaluation playbook | Voluntary guidance |
| ISO/IEC 5338:2023 | AI system lifecycle processes | Lifecycle roles, change gates, records | Complement software lifecycle standards |
| EU AI Act | Legal duties for applicable AI roles / uses | Legal classification, conformity workstream | Applicability and deadlines require counsel |
| OWASP 2026 guides | LLM / agent security threats & controls | Threat model, test cases, risk register | Community guidance, not regulation |

> **Decision / principle:** Standards translation should yield testable controls and recorded decisions, not a redundant set of documents.

# Build an evidence-based evaluation program tied to intended use

**Testing must be representative, adversarial and repeatable**

| Evaluation family | Representative tests | Evidence captured | Re-test trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task quality | Golden tasks, groundedness, calibration | Versioned dataset + scores | Model / prompt / data change |
| Safety and misuse | Disallowed requests, sensitive output | Attack results and mitigations | New threat or jailbreak class |
| Security / agents | Injection, tool escalation, exfiltration | Attack trace + permission evidence | Tool or access change |

| Human factors | Overrides, explanations, reliance tests | Review usability + escalation logs | Workflow / role redesign |

> **Decision / principle:** Do not reduce trust to a single LLM-as-judge score; evaluate with human adjudication and domain-specific thresholds.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 02 / AGENTIC CONTROL" -->

# Agentic AI requires controls on actions, not only responses

**Autonomy creates a distinct control plane**

### Before action

Identity-bound agents; scoped tools; policy check; transaction limits; approval for irreversible actions; sandbox for code execution.

### During action

Bounded recursion and budgets; context isolation; tool output validation; replayable traces; cancellation and rate limits.

### After action

Signed result/evidence, anomaly detection, user recourse, rollback or compensation, incident investigation and permission review.

> **Decision / principle:** Keep MCP / A2A interoperability separate from trust authorization; protocol support is not proof of safe delegation.

# Run Trusted AI as a recurring risk-and-assurance discipline

**Business ownership with independent second-line challenge**

### Weekly

New system intake, material changes, exceptions, red-team findings, critical evaluation failures, approved remediation SLAs.

### Monthly / quarterly

Risk committee, model/vendor concentration, inventory reconciliation, control health, training attestations, reassessment schedule.

### Event-triggered

Security incident, new use case, substantial model/tool change, data-source shift, performance regression, regulatory change.

> **Decision / principle:** The practice should own methods and independent testing; the business remains accountable for use-case outcomes and residual risk.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 02 / GOTCHAS" -->

# Trusted AI fails when governance becomes paperwork or theater

**Important limitations that belong in policy and contracts**

### False comfort

ISO/IEC 42001 certification certifies an AI management system within scope; it does not certify every model is safe or compliant.

### Evaluation limits

Benchmarks can leak or overfit; LLM judges disagree; rare harms are under-sampled; adversarial tests do not prove future safety.

### Operational gaps

Third-party model opacity, jurisdictional change, data residency and human over-reliance require explicit exceptions and monitoring.

> **Decision / principle:** No silent approvals: unresolved severe risks, missing data provenance and unowned agents block production access.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 03 / MANAGED AI OPERATIONS & ASSURANCE" -->

# Make AI reliability, cost and control observable every day

**Practice executive brief**

### Why it matters

Periodic audits cannot catch dynamic model behavior, prompt changes, tool integrations, data drift, incidents and operational cost spikes.

### What changes

Create a managed service with SLOs, continuous evals, telemetry, risk triggers, incident workflows and shared accountability.

### Where scale comes from

Multi-tenant monitoring patterns, common schemas, automated triage, policy packs, response playbooks and reusable customer reporting.

> **Decision / principle:** Commercialize service-level evidence and accountable response—not a dashboard subscription without remedial ownership.

<!-- _header: "PRACTICE 03 / COMMERCIAL MODEL" -->

# Package managed assurance into three defensible service tiers

**Service scale depends on standard onboarding plus risk-adjusted scope**

### Observe | foundational

Inventory reconciliation, basic traces, latency / cost alerts, standard reports and monthly control check. Best for low-risk use.

### Assure | enhanced

Continuous evaluations, security signals, exception workflow, risk reviews, drift checks, incident coordination. Best for moderate risk.

### Protect | critical

High-intensity detection, dedicated runbooks, change re-approval, red-team cadence, on-call and executive evidence. Best for higher risk.

> **Decision / principle:** Price by monitored service, traffic / telemetry band, control complexity and support window; explicitly meter expensive evals.

# Managed AI assurance has hard operational boundaries

**Protect service margin and avoid misleading risk transfer claims**

### Shared responsibility

Provider observes and responds within contract; customer remains accountable for business decisions, source data, tool permissions and legal duties.

### Evidence limits

Telemetry sampling loses rare events; drift alerts can be noisy; traces may include sensitive data; nondeterministic output complicates incident replay.

### Margin constraints

Continuous LLM evaluations, storage, high-volume traces, multilingual coverage and after-hours support can overwhelm fixed-price retainers.

> **Decision / principle:** Do not commit 24×7 remediation, zero hallucinations or guaranteed compliance without staffing, scope and measurable acceptance terms.
